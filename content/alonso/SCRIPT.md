# How Alonso could have been a 5-time champion — script v1

Format: illustrated data documentary in the style of the Dugout Kohli reel, about 95 s, 1080×1920.
Helmets instead of faces: no portraits of real drivers. Alonso's helmet is repainted in each team's colours, chapter by chapter.
Voiceover: ~250 words at an even, slightly urgent pace. Every number below is checked against Jolpica race results (`data/`).

**The running graphic.** A "POINTS FROM THE TITLE" bar chart on cream paper, with a dashed line at zero labelled CHAMPION. It gains one bar per chapter: 2005 **+21** and 2006 **+13** sit above the line in gold; 2007 **−1**, 2010 **−4** and 2012 **−3** sit just below it in red, so small they barely show. That is the point.

## Checked facts (Jolpica)
- 2005: Alonso 133, Räikkönen 112 (Renault). 2006: Alonso 134, Schumacher 121.
- 2007: Räikkönen 110, Hamilton 109, Alonso 109. Hungary: grid 6, finished 4th. Japan: accident, 0 pts. Before Brazil: Hamilton 107, Alonso 103, Räikkönen 100.
- 2010: before Abu Dhabi: Alonso 246, Webber 238, Vettel 231. Finished 7th (grid 3). Final: Vettel 256, Alonso 252. 4th place (12 pts) would have given 258.
- 2012: lead over Vettel peaked at +44 after Germany (round 10). Spa: collision, 0 pts (lead to 24). Suzuka: collision, 0 pts (lead to 4). Final: Vettel 281, Alonso 278.
- Career: 32 wins, last at the 2013 Spanish GP. 449 race entries. Born 29 July 1981.

## Notes
- "Youngest ever champion" is true as of 2005 only (Hamilton and Vettel later beat it), so the stamp says (2005).
- The penalty, the Petrov stint and the Grosjean crash are well documented in race reports. The rest comes straight from the results data.
