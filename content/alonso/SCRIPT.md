# The champion who should have won five: Fernando Alonso — script v2

About 2 min 40 s, 1080×1920. The whole career told as a story, with the 1, 4 and 3 points as the spine.
**Visual language:** real photos (openly licensed, credited) turned into comic print with halftone dots, on torn paper cards, mixed with drawn graphics (helmet, stamps, tapes, counters) and the running "points from the title" chart. The background colour changes with each era.
**Sources:** F1 numbers are checked against the official results (Jolpica, `data/`). Facts marked † are well known from race coverage but could not be checked here, because Wikipedia is blocked in this environment.

### 1 · Origins (0:08–0:30) — Asturias blue
> Oviedo, northern Spain. His father, a mechanic who raced karts for fun, built a kart for Fernando's older sister. She didn't want it. Fernando, aged three, did.† By fifteen he was junior world karting champion.† Formula Nissan champion at eighteen.† And in 2001, at nineteen, he was on the Formula One grid, with Minardi. He scored zero points.

Visual: halftone photo of his real 1996 world-championship kart (Museo Fernando Alonso). Tapes "Oviedo · 1981", "age 3", "1996: junior world champion". Counter **0 points**, stamped **MINARDI 2001**.

### 2 · Renault: the giants (0:30–0:58) — Renault blue and yellow
> Renault saw something. In 2003 he wins in Hungary, the youngest winner Formula One had ever seen.† Then he takes on the two giants of his time. Michael Schumacher: seven world titles. Kimi Räikkönen: the Iceman, in a McLaren that was often the fastest car on the grid. In 2005, Alonso beats Räikkönen to the title. In 2006, he beats Schumacher, head to head. Two titles by twenty-five. Everyone expected a dynasty.

Visual: three halftone photo cards dealt like trading cards: **SCHUMACHER · 7 TITLES**, **RÄIKKÖNEN · THE ICEMAN**, **ALONSO**. Counters **133 v 112**, then **134 v 121**. Chart: +21, +13. Tape "a dynasty?"

### 3 · McLaren 2007: the rookie (0:58–1:26) — McLaren silver
> 2007. The double champion joins McLaren. His teammate: a rookie called Lewis Hamilton. Alonso expects to lead. Instead, Hamilton finishes on the podium in each of his first nine races. The garage splits. In Hungary, Alonso holds Hamilton up in the pit lane and is moved from pole to sixth. In Japan, he crashes out in the rain. And in Brazil, Räikkönen steals the title from both of them. Alonso misses it by one point. After one season, he leaves.

Visual: halftone photo cards of Alonso and Hamilton (2007) side by side. A row of nine podium dots for Hamilton. The Hungary card ("P1 → P6", **PENALTY**), the Japan card (**DNF**), the final table 110 / 109 / 109. Chart: −1. Stamp **ONE SEASON**.

### 4 · Ferrari: dragging the car (1:26–2:05) — Ferrari red
> 2010. Ferrari. Against him: Sebastian Vettel, and a Red Bull that was the fastest car of the era. Abu Dhabi, the final race. Alonso leads the championship. Fourth would be enough. Ferrari pits him early, he comes out behind Petrov's Renault, and he never gets past. Seventh. Vettel is champion by four points.
> 2012 is his masterpiece. His Ferrari starts sixth on average. He finishes third on average, with thirteen podiums and more than double his teammate's points. He leads by forty-four in July. Then two first-lap crashes, at Spa and Suzuka. Vettel wins it by three.

Visual: halftone photo of Alonso's Ferrari leading Hamilton and Vettel (2012), and of Vettel's Red Bull. The 2010 table (246 / 238 / 231), "4th is enough", the Petrov train, **P7**. For 2012: **GRID 6.1 → FINISH 3.3**, **278 v 122** (Massa), the lead line +44 → DNF Spa → DNF Suzuka → −3. Chart: −4, −3.

### 5 · McLaren-Honda: the wilderness (2:05–2:18) — orange and black
> 2015. Back to McLaren, now with Honda. It's a disaster. In Japan he says it on the radio: "GP2 engine."† Four seasons. Zero podiums. In 2018, he walks away from Formula One.

Visual: halftone photo of the McLaren-Honda. Quote card **"GP2 ENGINE!"**. Counter **0 podiums · 4 seasons**.

## Checked numbers (Jolpica)
- Debut: 2001 Australian GP, Minardi, 0 points that season. Born 29 July 1981, so 19 at his debut.
- First win: 2003 Hungarian GP. 2005: 133 v Räikkönen 112. 2006: 134 v Schumacher 121.
- 2007: Hamilton's first nine finishes were 3, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1, 1, 3, 3. Hungary: grid 6, finished 4th. Japan: accident. Final: Räikkönen 110, Hamilton 109, Alonso 109.
- 2010: before Abu Dhabi, Alonso 246, Webber 238, Vettel 231. Finished 7th from 3rd on the grid. Final: Vettel 256, Alonso 252.
- 2012: average grid 6.1, average finish 3.3 (classified races), 13 podiums, 278 points v Massa 122. Lead +44 after Germany, Spa DNF, Suzuka DNF. Final: Vettel 281, Alonso 278.
- 2015–2018 (McLaren): 0 podiums. Japan 2015: finished 11th.
- 2021: Qatar GP 3rd (aged 40). 2023: 8 podiums. 2026: 7 points from 21 races. Career: 449 entries, 32 wins, last at the 2013 Spanish GP. Monaco wins: 2006, 2007.
